# Small Voxel Fragments / Debris in Physics Simulation: Why CCD Is a Disaster and What Production Games Do

**Scope note:** Sections are tagged **[VERIFIED]** (read directly from a primary source — engine source code, official docs, or a dev's own blog/interview) or **[INFERENCE]** (my analysis, derived from verified facts). Every verified claim carries its URL. Where I could not find a source, I say so explicitly rather than filling the gap.

---

## 0. Executive summary

1. **The tunneling condition is size-relative, not speed-relative.** A body tunnels when its per-step motion exceeds its own thinnest extent. Every major engine therefore gates CCD behind a per-body test of the form \`motion > k · thinnest_extent\` with \`k = 0.5\`. **[VERIFIED]**
2. **That threshold is exactly backwards for debris.** Because the threshold scales with *size*, the smaller the fragment, the *lower* the speed at which it becomes permanently CCD-eligible. A 1-voxel fragment is CCD-active at walking pace, forever. This is the core problem, and PhysX documents it explicitly as a failure mode. **[VERIFIED]**
3. **No production engine fixes tunneling by globally substepping the world.** Every engine instead: (a) computes a per-body "fast" flag, (b) does a per-body swept query (shape cast) only for those bodies, (c) restricts what those sweeps can hit, and (d) caps velocity so the required sweep length is bounded. **[VERIFIED]**
4. **Global substepping is the wrong tool because its cost is \`N × (entire pipeline)\` while per-body CCD is \`(fast bodies) × (one sweep each)\`.** With a handful of fragments among thousands of bodies that is roughly two orders of magnitude difference. **[INFERENCE, derived from verified engine structure]**
5. **For 1–3 voxel fragments, production practice is not to simulate them faithfully at all.** Teardown *removes* debris below a minimum size and exposes per-shape collision filters; Noita keeps rigid bodies as separate convex-primitive entities and never promotes loose pixels to rigid bodies. **[VERIFIED]**

## 1. The actual cost model

### 1.1 The tunneling condition

A discrete-step simulator samples position at \`t\` and \`t+dt\`. A body of thinnest extent \`t_ext\` moving at speed \`v\` is missed entirely when:

\`\`\`
v · dt  >  t_ext          (tunneling condition)
\`\`\`

The required global substep count to *guarantee* no tunneling is therefore:

\`\`\`
N  ≥  v · dt / t_ext
\`\`\`

This is the number that explodes for debris. **[INFERENCE — standard swept-collision reasoning, and the same quantity engines use for their fast-body test]**

### 1.2 Why global substepping is the wrong axis

**[INFERENCE, but grounded in the verified architecture of every engine below]**

- A substep is *not* a localized operation. Splitting \`dt\` into \`N\` pieces re-runs **broad phase, narrow phase, contact generation and the solver** over the entire active set, \`N\` times.
- Cost of global substepping: \`O( N · (B + P + S) )\` where \`B\` = active bodies, \`P\` = broad-phase pairs, \`S\` = solver work.
- Cost of per-body CCD: \`O( F · Q )\` where \`F\` = number of fast bodies and \`Q\` = one swept query over the swept AABB.
- With \`F = 20\` fragments in a \`B = 5000\`-body scene, \`F/B = 0.4%\`. The per-body approach touches a fraction of a percent of the scene; the global approach multiplies 100% of it by \`N\`.
- **Substeps are also global by necessity.** You cannot substep one body in isolation without either splitting its island (its neighbours then pay too) or decoupling it — which *is* per-body CCD. That is why no engine implements "global substepping for tunneling".

**Concrete numbers for the substep requirement** (\`dt = 1/60 s\`, one voxel = 0.1 m) **[INFERENCE — arithmetic]**:

| Fragment | Thinnest extent | Speed | Required N |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-voxel chunk | 0.3 m | 15 m/s | 3.3 → 4 |
| 1-voxel chip | 0.1 m | 15 m/s | 8.3 → 9 |
| 1-voxel chip | 0.1 m | 50 m/s | 27.8 → 28 |
| 1-voxel chip | 0.05 m | 100 m/s | 33.3 → 34 |

Every one of those \`N\` values is paid by **the entire world**, per frame, for **one** fragment.

### 1.3 The threshold makes tiny bodies permanently CCD-active **[VERIFIED]**

Rapier's fast-body criterion, from \`src/dynamics/rigid_body_components.rs\`:

\`\`\`rust
/// Is this rigid-body moving fast enough so that it may cause a tunneling problem?
///
/// The fast-body criterion: fast when the farthest point of its colliders can move more
/// than half the body's thinnest extent (\`ccd_thickness\`) within one timestep.
pub fn is_moving_fast(&self, dt: Real, vels: &RigidBodyVelocity<Real>, ...) -> bool {
    let max_point_velocity = ...;
    max_point_velocity * dt > Self::FAST_BODY_SAFETY_FACTOR * self.ccd_thickness
}

/// The fast-body safety factor: a body is fast when it can move more than half its
/// thinnest extent in one step.
pub const FAST_BODY_SAFETY_FACTOR: Real = 0.5;
\`\`\`
— <https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/master/src/dynamics/rigid_body_components.rs>

with \`max_point_velocity = |linvel| + |angvel| · max_extent\` (same file).

Box2D v3 uses the identical shape of test, from \`src/solver.c\`:

\`\`\`c
float safetyFactor = body->safetyFactor;
float maxMotion = b2MaxFloat( maxDeltaPosition, maxVelocity * timeStep );
if ( body->type == b2_dynamicBody && enableContinuous && maxMotion > safetyFactor * sim->minExtent )
{
    sim->flags |= b2_isFast;
    ...
}
\`\`\`
— <https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/main/src/solver.c>

and its documentation of the knob, from \`include/box2d/types.h\`:

> "Continuous collision safety factor. The solver only uses continuous collision if there is a risk of tunneling. If the body is moving fast enough to risk tunneling then it is considered a "fast body". This improves performance and prevents movement hitches. If a body moving N meter risks tunneling, then the body will be considered fast if it moves more than a safetyFactor times N meters over one full time step. Non-dimensional. **Recommended range [0.01, 0.5]. Default is 0.5 for high performance with low tunneling risk.**"
— <https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/main/include/box2d/types.h>

**Now plug in a 1-voxel fragment.** Threshold: \`v > 0.5 · t_ext / dt = 30 · t_ext\` at 60 Hz. **[INFERENCE — arithmetic on the verified formula]**

| Body | Thinnest extent | Speed needed to be "fast" |
|---|---|---|
| Building chunk | 2.0 m | 60 m/s |
| Crate | 0.5 m | 15 m/s |
| **1-voxel chip** | **0.1 m** | **3 m/s** |
| **1-voxel chip** | **0.05 m** | **1.5 m/s** |

A 1-voxel fragment reaches 3 m/s after ~0.3 s of free fall. **Every falling debris fragment in the game is permanently CCD-active.** CCD stops being an occasional event cost and becomes a permanent per-body tax on precisely the objects you have the most of.

### 1.4 PhysX documents this exact failure mode **[VERIFIED]**

This is the single best primary-source statement of the problem:

> "The CCD system is a best-effort conservative advancement scheme. It runs a finite number of CCD substeps (defaulted to 1) and drops any remaining time. Usually, time is only dropped on high-speed objects at the moment of impact so it is not noticeable. **However, this artefact can become noticeable if you simulate an object that is sufficiently small/thin relative to the simulation time-step that the object could tunnel if it was accelerated by gravity from rest for 1 frame, i.e. a paper-thin rigid body. Such an object would always be moving at above its CCD velocity threshold and could result in a large proportion of simulation time being dropped for that object and any objects in the same island as it (any objects whose bounds overlap the bounds of that object). This could cause a noticeable slow-down/stuttering effect caused by the objects in that island becoming noticeably out-of-sync with the rest of the simulation. It is therefore recommended that paper-thin/tiny objects should be avoided if possible.**"
— PhysX 5.4, *Advanced Collision Detection → Limitations*, <https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.0/docs/AdvancedCollisionDetection.html>

Note the **island amplification**: the damage is not confined to the tiny body — it contaminates everything whose bounds overlap it. In a debris pile that is all of the debris. **[VERIFIED — stated in the quote]**

Also from the same page:

> "CCD only activates between shapes whose relative speeds are above the sum of their respective CCD velocity thresholds. **These velocity thresholds are automatically calculated based on the shape's properties** and support non-uniform scales."

> "Enabling CCD on a scene/all bodies in a scene should be relatively efficient but it will have some performance impact even when all the objects in the scene are moving relatively slowly. ... **As the objects' velocities increase, the CCD overhead will increase, especially if there are a lot of high-speed objects in close proximity.** Increasing the number of CCD passes can make the CCD more expensive although the CCD will terminate early if the additional passes are not required."

> "\`PxSceneDesc::ccdMaxPasses\`: this variable controls the number of CCD passes performed by PhysX. **This is defaulted to 1** ... Increasing this value permits the CCD to run multiple passes. This reduces the likelihood of time being dropped but **can increase the cost of the CCD**."

> "\`PxRigidBody::setMinCCDAdvanceCoefficient()\`: ... By default, this value is **0.15**, meaning that CCD will advance an object by the 0.15 * ccdThreshold ... **Reducing this value can negatively impact fluidity** but will reduce the likelihood of objects clipping at the end of a frame. Increasing this value may increase the likelihood of objects tunnelling."

Note also: **"In close proximity"** is the debris-pile case. CCD cost is driven by the *swept* region, and a debris pile puts many sweep targets in one place. **[INFERENCE on the last clause; the "close proximity" phrasing is verified]**

### 1.5 Swept volumes poison the broad phase **[VERIFIED — from Rapier's own source comment]**

Rapier's CCD solver carries an explicit warning about the second-order cost of sweeps:

> "NOTE: broad-phase AABBs are NOT enlarged to the swept volumes: only the fast body's query box is swept (per collider, below); targets keep their regular fat AABBs. **Swept AABBs written into the tree would leak into the next step (pair explosion, ~2x narrow-phase cost).**"

and it caches the fixed-target list specifically because scanning was the bottleneck:

> "Cached fixed-target list for the non-bullet sweep pass ... Invalidated by the scene-change flag — **re-scanning every collider each step dominated CCD on large scenes.**"

and, on the algorithm change:

> "**Rework the CCD logic to something similar to Box2D v3's approach. The rationale is that their CCD implementations work very well and with very low overhead.**"
— Rapier PR #973, "feat: significant performance and stability improvements", <https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/pull/973>
— <https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/master/src/dynamics/ccd/ccd_solver.rs>

### 1.6 A real-world CCD cost blow-up **[VERIFIED]**

Box2D issue #508 ("Major random bug causes the engine to quickly go on a downward spiral until it crashes") reports profiler output where \`solveTOI\` goes from **0.16–0.59 ms** in normal operation to **136 ms**, and eventually **136043 ms**, while the broad-phase pair count goes from ~138 to **951895**. The reporter's own instrumented log, quoted from the issue:

{PR|5.61|1.56|3.11|0.25|0.21|0.16|0.55|1.08}   (ms of step|collide|solve|solveInit|solveVelocity|solvePosition|solveTOI|broadphase)
{94||194}                                        (moveCount||pairCount)
...
{PR|247.14|16.28|94.44|0.44|0.27|0.21|136.07|90.47}

\`\`\`
— <https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/issues/508>

This is a bug case, not a steady state, but it demonstrates the cost structure: **TOI work scales with the number of pairs inside the swept region, and that number can run away.**

### 1.7 Time loss and visual artifacts **[VERIFIED]**

CCD clamps the body to its time of impact; the remaining time is discarded. Jolt documents the visual consequence:

> "**Time is stolen from the object** (which means it will move up to the first collision and will not bounce off the surface until the next integration step). **This will make the body appear to go slower when it collides with high velocity.** In order to not get stuck, the body is always allowed to move by a fraction of it's inner radius, **which may eventually lead it to pass through geometry.**"
— Jolt \`Jolt/Physics/Body/MotionQuality.h\`, <https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/master/Jolt/Physics/Body/MotionQuality.h>

For 1-voxel debris this is doubly bad: the fragment *always* trips the threshold, so it *always* loses time, so debris visibly stalls — and the "allowed to move by a fraction of its inner radius" escape hatch is a fraction of an *already tiny* radius, so it can still squeeze through.

## 2. The standard alternative: per-body swept query / shape cast

All five engines implement the same shape of solution. The pattern is:

**(a) per-body fast test → (b) per-body sweep → (c) restricted target set → (d) velocity cap.**

### 2.1 Rapier — "motion clamping", two tiers **[VERIFIED]**

> "Continuous Collision Detection (CCD) is used to make sure that fast-moving objects don't miss any contacts (a problem usually called **tunneling**). This is done by looking for collisions along the shapes motion: a rigid-body that moved fast during the timestep casts its colliders from their previous position to their new one, and **its position is clamped to the first impact found this way. Its velocities are left untouched and the solver is responsible for preventing penetrations.** Since the trajectory is clamped to some intermediate location along its path this technique is commonly called **motion clamping**."

> "Rapier applies CCD in two ways:
> - Every fast-moving dynamic rigid-body is swept against the **fixed** colliders and the soft-bodies of the scene. **This is automatic and doesn't need to be enabled** ... **Note however that this is not enabled for dynamic rigid-bodies with mesh-like shapes (triangle meshes, polylines, heightfields) as that would be too computationally expensive.**
> - A dynamic rigid-body with CCD enabled (aka. a **bullet**) is swept against the **kinematic and dynamic** bodies as well. **This is more expensive, therefore it is disabled by default and should be reserved to the objects that must not tunnel through moving obstacles.** Note that two CCD-enabled objects might still tunnel since the CCD resolution does currently not take both continuous motions into account simultaneously."

> "**CCD takes action only if the rigid-body is moving fast relative to another collider. Therefore it is useless to enable it on fixed rigid-bodies and on rigid-bodies that are expected to move slowly.**"

> "The CCD feature, including the automatic sweeping of the fast dynamic bodies, can be fully disabled by setting the maximum number of CCD substeps to zero ... (the \`max_ccd_substeps\` field, **whose default is 1**). **Larger values let a body resolve several successive impacts within a single timestep, at the cost of additional sweeps.**"
— <https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/python/rigid_body_ccd/>

Rapier's defaults, from \`src/dynamics/integration_parameters.rs\` (<https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/master/src/dynamics/integration_parameters.rs>):

\`\`\`rust
/// Maximum number of CCD substeps performed by the solver (default: 1).
/// Also the global CCD on/off switch: 0 disables **all** CCD for the world
pub max_ccd_substeps: usize,

/// The maximal distance separating two objects that will generate predictive contacts (default: 0.02).
pub normalized_prediction_distance: Real,

/// Geometric slop distance (default: 0.005), e.g. the standoff kept by the CCD clamp.
pub normalized_allowed_linear_error: Real,

/// Maximum speed at which contact penetration is pushed out by the biased solve (default: 3.0).
pub normalized_max_corrective_velocity: Real,

/// Maximum linear velocity a body may have after each solver substep (default: 400.0 m/s).
/// Bounding per-step travel keeps CCD and speculative contacts
/// reliable (a body cannot be flung or crushed to an arbitrary speed); set to Real::MAX to disable.
pub normalized_max_linear_velocity: Real,
\`\`\`

Plus an angular velocity cap, documented on the CCD component:

> "Allow this body to exceed the angular speed cap. **By default angular velocity is clamped each substep to ~45°/step to keep CCD reliable**; set true for bodies that must spin fast (e.g. wheels)."
— \`RigidBodyCcd::allow_fast_rotation\`, <https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/master/src/dynamics/rigid_body_components.rs>

And a residual-contact mechanism:

> "after the solver, bodies that moved more than half their thinnest extent sweep their colliders and \`next_position\` is clamped to the earliest impact — velocities untouched, no re-solve; **the residual approach resolves next step via speculative contacts.**"
— <https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/master/src/dynamics/ccd/ccd_solver.rs>

**The takeaway for debris:** the *automatic* tier sweeps only against fixed geometry, runs in parallel, and uses a **cached list of fixed targets**. For a fragment hitting the ground that is exactly the needed query, and it never touches the debris pile. **[VERIFIED structure; the "for a fragment" framing is INFERENCE]**

### 2.2 Box2D v3 — per-body \`b2_isFast\`, static-only by default **[VERIFIED]**

Only fast bodies enter \`b2SolveContinuous\`, and non-bullets query **only the static tree**:

\`\`\`c
b2DynamicTree* staticTree = world->broadPhase.trees + b2_staticBody;
b2DynamicTree* kinematicTree = world->broadPhase.trees + b2_kinematicBody;
b2DynamicTree* dynamicTree = world->broadPhase.trees + b2_dynamicBody;
...
b2DynamicTree_Query( staticTree, sweptBox, B2_DEFAULT_MASK_BITS, b2ContinuousQueryCallback, &context );
if ( isBullet ) {
    b2DynamicTree_Query( kinematicTree, sweptBox, B2_DEFAULT_MASK_BITS, b2ContinuousQueryCallback, &context );
    b2DynamicTree_Query( dynamicTree, sweptBox, B2_DEFAULT_MASK_BITS, b2ContinuousQueryCallback, &context );
}
\`\`\`
— <https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/main/src/solver.c>

and the docs:

> "By default, Box2D uses continuous collision detection (CCD) to prevent dynamic bodies from tunneling **through static bodies**. ... **Normally CCD is not used between dynamic bodies. This is done to keep performance reasonable.**"
— <https://box2d.org/documentation/md_simulation.html>

**The strongest statement in any engine doc about what to do instead of CCD for projectiles/debris:**

> "\`isBullet\`: Treat this body as a high speed object that performs continuous collision detection against dynamic and kinematic bodies, but not other bullet bodies. **@warning Bullets should be used sparingly. They are not a solution for general dynamic-versus-dynamic continuous collision.** They do not guarantee accurate collision if both bodies are fast moving because the bullet does a continuous check after all non-bullet bodies have moved. ... **I do not recommend using them for game projectiles if precise collision timing is needed. Instead consider using a ray or shape cast. You can use a marching ray or shape cast for projectile that moves over time.** If you want a fast moving projectile to collide with a fast moving target, you need to consider the relative movement in your ray or shape cast. This is out of the scope of Box2D."
— <https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/main/include/box2d/types.h>

Also, Box2D's \`solver.c\` has a \`b2_isSpeedCapped\` body flag and re-centres the sweep on the fast body "so the TOI and the swept query stay in float precision" — a detail that matters a lot when fragments are tiny and the world is large. **[VERIFIED]**

### 2.3 Bullet — swept **sphere** + per-body motion threshold **[VERIFIED]**

> "\`setCcdMotionThreshold\`: **Don't do continuous collision detection if the motion (in one step) is less then m_ccdMotionThreshold**"
> "\`setCcdSweptSphereRadius\`: Swept sphere radius (0.0 by default)"
— \`btCollisionObject.h\`, <https://github.com/bulletphysics/bullet3/blob/master/src/BulletCollision/CollisionDispatch/btCollisionObject.h>

The implementation shows the cost model concretely — Bullet approximates the body by a **sphere** and does one \`convexSweepTest\` per fast body:

\`\`\`cpp
btScalar squareMotion = (predictedTrans.getOrigin() - body->getWorldTransform().getOrigin()).length2();
if (getDispatchInfo().m_useContinuous && body->getCcdSquareMotionThreshold()
        && body->getCcdSquareMotionThreshold() < squareMotion) {
    if (body->getCollisionShape()->isConvex()) {          // <-- non-convex shapes are skipped
        btSphereShape tmpSphere(body->getCcdSweptSphereRadius());
        sweepResults.m_allowedPenetration = getDispatchInfo().m_allowedCcdPenetration;
        convexSweepTest(&tmpSphere, body->getWorldTransform(), modifiedPredictedTrans, sweepResults);
        ...
    }
}
\`\`\`
— \`createPredictiveContactsInternal\`, <https://github.com/bulletphysics/bullet3/blob/master/src/BulletDynamics/Dynamics/btDiscreteDynamicsWorld.cpp>

Note: **\`m_ccdMotionThreshold\` defaults to 0, which means CCD is off** — you must opt in per body. And the sweep is against a *sphere* of your chosen radius, not the real shape. **[VERIFIED]**

### 2.4 Jolt — motion quality is an explicit per-body enum **[VERIFIED]**

\`\`\`cpp
/// Motion quality, or how well it detects collisions when it has a high velocity
enum class EMotionQuality : uint8
{
    /// Update the body in discrete steps. Body will tunnel through thin objects if its velocity is high enough.
    /// **This is the cheapest way of simulating a body.**
    Discrete,

    /// Update the body using linear casting. ...
    LinearCast,
};
\`\`\`
— <https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/master/Jolt/Physics/Body/MotionQuality.h>

\`BodyCreationSettings::mMotionQuality\` **defaults to \`Discrete\`** (<https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/master/Jolt/Physics/Body/BodyCreationSettings.h>), and Jolt also bounds motion globally:

\`\`\`cpp
float mMaxLinearVelocity  = 500.0f;                  ///< Maximum linear velocity that this body can reach (m/s)
float mMaxAngularVelocity = 0.25f * JPH_PI * 60.0f;  ///< Maximum angular velocity that this body can reach (rad/s)
\`\`\`

### 2.5 PhysX — sweep-based vs speculative, and per-pair filtering **[VERIFIED]**

Three separate opt-ins are required (\`PxSceneFlag::eENABLE_CCD\`, \`PxPairFlag::eDETECT_CCD_CONTACT\` in the pair filter, and \`PxRigidBodyFlag::eENABLE_CCD\` per body).

> "**Speculative CCD** ... In addition to the above sweep-based CCD, PhysX also provides a cheaper but less robust approach called speculative CCD. This approach functions differently to the sweep-based CCD in that it **operates entirely as part of the discrete simulation by inflating contact offsets based on object motion and depending on the constraint solver to ensure that objects do not tunnel through each-other.** Contrary to the sweep-based CCD, speculative CCD can catch missed contacts generated by fast angular motions. This is why the sweep-based CCD is sometimes called **linear CCD**, and speculative CCD is sometimes called **angular CCD**. ... However, there are cases where it can fail ... if the constraint solver accelerates an actor (as a result of a collision or joint) such that the actor passes entirely through objects during that time-step, speculative CCD can result in tunneling."

> "\`PxSceneFlag::eDISABLE_CCD_RESWEEP\`: enabling this flag disables CCD resweeps. **This can result in missed collisions as the result of ricochets but has the potential to reduce the overhead of the CCD.** In general, enabling this advancement mode still guarantees that objects will not pass through the static environment but no longer guarantees that dynamic objects with CCD enabled will not pass through each-other."

> "**It is also recommended that you filter away CCD interactions between bodies that are constrained together**, e.g. limbs in the same ragdoll. Allowing CCD interactions between limbs of the same ragdoll could increase the cost of CCD and also potentially cause time to be dropped unnecessarily."
— <https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.0/docs/AdvancedCollisionDetection.html>

That last quote generalizes directly: **filtering the CCD pair set is a first-class performance lever**, and it is the engine-endorsed way to stop debris-debris CCD from costing anything. **[INFERENCE on the generalization]**

## 3. What games actually do about 1–3 voxel fragments

### 3.1 Teardown

**Important honesty note:** I found **no primary source** in which Dennis Gustafsson says "Teardown does not use CCD" or explains avoiding it. Voxagon's blog has no CCD post; the 80.lv interview and the multiplayer devblog do not mention CCD. Treat the "why Teardown doesn't use CCD" framing as **unverified**. What I *can* document is the architecture and the debris policy.

**[VERIFIED] Voxel representation — dense grids, not an SVO:**

> "Teardown uses a **dense voxel format**, where every shape stores voxel data in an uncompressed, regular 3D grid. This is very fast to access, but since it's just a grid, regions of empty space within an object take up the same amount of memory as regions that actually contain voxels. This requires assets to be split up into multiple smaller shapes in order to get a better bounding box fit. **The downside of this is of course that you end up with a lot of shapes, which also has an overhead.** The new engine features a sparse voxel format that splits each shape into 8x8x8 voxel "chunks", which are tracked with a 3D bitmap."
— *Year summary*, 29 Dec 2024, <https://blog.voxagon.se/2024/12/29/year-summary.html>

Note this directly contradicts the widely-copied Russian article <https://media-xyz.com/ru/articles/714-razrushaemost-v-teardown-kak-sovmestit-geimple/> which claims Teardown is built on an SVO. **Do not cite that article for Teardown internals.**

**[VERIFIED] Collision detection is voxel-based:**

> "Teardown objects are represented and modeled as **voxel volumes on a regular grid**, and the whole engine has been built around that. This simplifies a lot of things both in physics and graphics when breaking them up."
> "**When I first started rewriting the collision detection to work with voxels, I thought it would be awfully slow, but it actually wasn't so bad as I initially thought.** During early access, I spent a lot of time optimizing the physics and destruction, making use of both SIMD and multithreading to take advantage of modern hardware."
— Dennis Gustafsson, 80.lv interview, 17 Mar 2026, <https://80.lv/articles/teardown-developer-breaks-down-multiplayer-and-voxel-destruction-tech>

**[VERIFIED] Teardown's *new* engine uses substepping + a parallel solver — a stability technique, not a tunneling fix:**

> "The new physics engine now uses **substepping instead of solver iteration** (a method described here and sometimes referred to as "Temporal Gauss Seidel") and features a **parallel solver that can solve large piles of objects on multiple threads**. There have also been improvements to contact generation and broad phase."
— *Year summary*, <https://blog.voxagon.se/2024/12/29/year-summary.html>

> "the system runs on a high-performance i9 CPU with NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 Ti using 32 threads and simulates in **approximately 5 ms**."
— 80.lv, <https://80.lv/articles/see-what-s-new-in-teardown-creator-s-custom-voxel-physics-engine>

**[VERIFIED] Teardown removes small debris below a minimum size.** A popular Teardown mod exists whose entire purpose is to defeat that behaviour:

> "**Small debris exceeding the established minimum will no longer be removed.** Because of this, destruction has a stronger cinematic sophistication **at the cost of performance.** ... **This mod is very demanding on performance with a large number of destructions.** Mods like spirit bomb or nuke can disable the game. **An insane amount of small debris can very quickly reach the engine cover [ceiling] and cause unstable physics. Use with caution.** The following options are enabled: **Minimum save size** and a switch if the fragments are rendered at a great distance."
— *Small Debris Retainer*, mod by Doomdrvk, mirror of the Nexus description: <https://www.modsgaming.us/load/teardown/mods/small_debris_retainer/179-1-0-99198> (original: <https://www.nexusmods.com/teardown/mods/385>)

This is a direct, third-party-confirmed statement that **(a) Teardown has a minimum debris size below which fragments are culled, and (b) lifting that cap collapses performance and destabilises the simulation.** It is the single most on-point Teardown data point in this report.

**[VERIFIED] Teardown exposes the per-shape collision filter and fragment topology ops in Lua.** From the official scripting API function list:

- Scene queries: \`QueryRaycast\`, \`QueryRaycastRope\`, \`QueryRaycastWater\`, **\`QueryShot\`**, \`QueryClosestPoint\`, \`QueryAabbShapes\`, \`QueryAabbBodies\`, \`QueryPath\`

- Body ops: \`SetBodyDynamic\`, \`IsBodyActive\`, \`SetBodyActive\`, \`ConstrainVelocity\`, \`ConstrainAngularVelocity\`, \`SetBodyVelocity\`, \`ApplyBodyImpulse\`, \`GetBodyMass\`
— <https://teardowngame.com/modding/api.html>

**\`QueryShot\` exists** and sits in the same scene-query family as the raycast and AABB queries. **I could not retrieve its prose description** — the official page exceeds the fetch limit (>100 kB, truncated at the Particles section) and every mirror I tried was blocked (Cloudflare on Nexus/Fandom/Stack Exchange; the \`teardown-api-docs-json\` file is 238 kB so the GitHub blob API truncates it too). **Do not assume it is a shape cast.** Name and grouping are the only verified facts. **[VERIFIED: existence and grouping. UNVERIFIED: semantics.]**

**What Teardown does *not* appear to do [INFERENCE]:** There is no CCD, sweep, or continuous-collision concept anywhere in the Lua API, and Gustafsson's own technical writing describes collision detection as voxel-grid-based plus solver improvements (TGS substepping, parallel solver, better contact generation and broad phase). The consistent picture is: **discrete voxel-overlap collision + a fixed timestep + aggressive culling of small fragments + per-shape collision filtering**, rather than making the collision detection continuous. A voxel-grid overlap test between a 1-voxel chip and a wall is a few cell comparisons; wrapping each of thousands of chips in a swept query would be far more expensive than the collision test itself. That is a coherent engineering rationale, **but I am inferring it — I have no statement from the developer confirming it.**

### 3.2 Noita

**[VERIFIED] Rigid bodies and pixels are two separate systems.** Noita's rigid bodies are entities with **convex primitive** collision shapes, not pixel grids:

> "\`collision_shape\` | string | Defines the shape of the collision body. **Common values include "box", "circle", "capsule".**" ... "\`is_static\`", "\`is_kinematic\`", "\`mass\`", "\`friction\`", "\`restitution\`", "\`linear_damping\`", "\`angular_damping\`", "\`gravity_scale\`", "\`enable_impact_damage\`", "\`impact_damage_min_force\`"
— Noita \`PhysicsBodyComponent\` documentation, <https://noita.wiki.gg/wiki/Documentation:PhysicsBodyComponent> (mirrored at <https://github.com/DarrenRM/Noita-Context7-Docs/blob/master/modding-wiki/physicsbodycomponent.md>)

**[VERIFIED] Pixels are a falling-sand grid simulation, multithreaded with dirty rectangles:**

> "Noita uses a very simple falling sand simulation algorithm, liquids and gases are implemented similarly. **Rigid bodies use a marching square algorithm.** How to simulate all pixels in a big world? **Multi-threading! The world is divided into 64×64 chunks. Each chunk keeps a dirty rectangle, containing all the pixels that need to be simulated.**"
— notes on the GDC 2019 talk *Exploring the Tech and Design of 'Noita'* by Petri Purho, <https://github.com/jethrokuan/braindump/blob/master/content/posts/gdc_vault_exploring_the_tech_and_design_of_noita.md>

The talk's own abstract confirms the framing:

> "'Noita' is a rogue-lite set in a procedurally generated physics based destructible world. In this talk, co-creator Petri Purho talks about the technical details of the physics engine, including **scaling up the falling sand simulation to support large continuous worlds and integrating destructible rigid body physics**."
— <https://gdcvault.com/play/1025695/Exp>

**The architectural point [INFERENCE, but strongly supported]:** Noita never promotes loose pixels to rigid bodies. A pixel is a grid cell simulated by the falling-sand pass (cheap, cell-local, dirty-rect bounded). A rigid body is a small number of entities with box/circle/capsule shapes handled by the rigid-body solver. So "1-pixel debris" is not a physics body at all — it is a grid cell. This sidesteps the entire tiny-fast-body CCD problem by **not having tiny fast bodies**. The "marching squares" note is consistent with tracing pixel-grid contours to build/resolve rigid-body collisions against terrain, but I am **not** claiming the exact mechanism.

## 4. Concrete playbook for small voxel debris

Ordered by cost/benefit, with the source backing each item.

| # | Technique | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cull fragments below a size/voxel-count threshold.** Don't simulate 1–3 voxel chips at all; delete them or convert to particles. | Teardown: "Small debris exceeding the established minimum will no longer be removed" — i.e. vanilla *does* remove it. Removing the cap "can quickly reach the engine ceiling and cause unstable physics." <https://www.modsgaming.us/load/teardown/mods/small_debris_retainer/179-1-0-99198> |
| 2 | **Restrict debris collisions to static world only.** Debris-vs-debris is the pair explosion; debris-vs-world is the query you can cache and parallelise. | Rapier's automatic tier sweeps **fixed colliders only**; the fixed-target list is cached because "re-scanning every collider each step dominated CCD on large scenes." Box2D v3 non-bullets query **only the static tree**. <https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/master/src/dynamics/ccd/ccd_solver.rs>, <https://github.com/erincatto/box2d/blob/main/src/solver.c> |
| 3 | **Expose and use a per-object collision filter/mask.** | Teardown Lua: \`SetShapeCollisionFilter\` / \`GetShapeCollisionFilter\`. Jolt: "Ensure that your sensor is in an **object layer that collides with as few static bodies as possible**." <https://teardowngame.com/modding/api.html>, <https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/> |
| 4 | **Size-based CCD gating, not "CCD on/off for the world".** Use \`motion > 0.5 · thinnest_extent\`. | Rapier \`FAST_BODY_SAFETY_FACTOR = 0.5\`; Box2D v3 \`safetyFactor\` default 0.5, range [0.01, 0.5]. Sources above. |
| 5 | **Cap velocity so per-step travel is bounded.** This bounds the swept volume, which bounds every downstream cost. | Rapier: max linear velocity 400 m/s default, "**Bounding per-step travel keeps CCD and speculative contacts reliable**"; angular clamped to ~45°/step. Jolt: \`mMaxLinearVelocity = 500\`, \`mMaxAngularVelocity = 0.25π·60\`. |
| 6 | **Use speculative/predictive contacts instead of sweeps for the general case.** Cheaper, handles angular motion, needs no sweep at all. | PhysX: "speculative CCD ... operates entirely as part of the discrete simulation by **inflating contact offsets based on object motion** ... sweep-based CCD is sometimes called linear CCD, and speculative CCD is sometimes called angular CCD." Rapier: \`normalized_prediction_distance\` default 0.02. |
| 7 | **Shape-cast / ray-cast the fragment yourself rather than relying on body CCD** — especially for projectiles and thrown debris. | Box2D v3: "**I do not recommend using them for game projectiles if precise collision timing is needed. Instead consider using a ray or shape cast.** You can use a marching ray or shape cast for projectile that moves over time." Bullet's implementation is literally \`btSphereShape(ccdSweptSphereRadius)\` + \`convexSweepTest\`. |
| 8 | **Merge fragments into compound bodies / rebuild connected components.** | octo changelog: "rigidbody physics system with **connected component detection**." Teardown Lua: \`MergeShape\`, \`SplitShape\`, \`TrimShape\`, \`IsShapeDisconnected\`, \`GetShapeVoxelCount\`. |
| 9 | **Sleep islands aggressively; deactivate distant debris.** | Jolt: "At the end of each step, all the bodies in an island are checked to see if they have come to rest, if this is the case then the **entire island is put to sleep**. When a body is sleeping, ... it will not move or otherwise participate in the simulation to conserve CPU cycles." Bullet: "Deactivated (sleeping) rigid bodies don't take any processing time, except a minor broadphase collision detection impact." |
| 10 | **Parallelise the sweep pass over fast bodies only.** | Rapier: \`map_bodies_parallel\` over fast bodies, two passes (non-bullets, then bullets). Box2D v3: fast bodies are handled in the task system; bullets are deferred to a separate deterministic stage. |
| 11 | **Do not globally substep to fix tunneling.** | No engine does this. Both substep-like knobs that exist are **per-body sweep counts**: Rapier \`max_ccd_substeps\` default 1 ("at the cost of additional sweeps"), PhysX \`ccdMaxPasses\` default 1 ("can increase the cost of the CCD"). Rapier's TGS substepping (as adopted by Teardown's new engine) is a *stability/convergence* technique. |

### 4.1 The decision rule, stated concretely **[INFERENCE]**

\`\`\`
for each dynamic body:
    motion = (|v| + |ω| · max_extent) * dt
    if motion > 0.5 * thinnest_extent:
        # this body needs continuous handling
        if it is a fragment (small thinnest_extent):
            # it will satisfy this test almost always -> do NOT sweep it every frame
            -> cull it, merge it, or move it to a non-colliding layer
        else:
            -> shape cast against static geometry only
\`\`\`

The reason fragments get special-cased is structural: for a 1-voxel chip the test is **always true**, so a naive implementation pays a sweep per chip per frame forever. That is the trap. Engines protect themselves from it by making the *test* size-relative, and games protect themselves by never letting a 1-voxel chip become a simulated body.
